CURRENT SCULPTURES (2026-11-10 gold-style correction)

The active scene uses the existing buddha.glb, avalokitesvara.glb and
mahastamaprapta.glb development assets, credited in assets/sources.json.
The three main dedication positions share one base Buddha sculpture; they are
application staging, not three independently sourced/authenticated portrait models.
Side rear positions now use the same bodhisattva family, not an arhat collection.
New matching lotus pedestals are application-created geometry.
The three scans below are ARCHIVED RESEARCH, not loaded or included in the
current portable scene. Their original/derivative files retain their licences.

ARCHIVED MAIN/SIDE SCULPTURES (prior version)

dipankara.glb: "Stele with Buddha Dipankara (Randeng)", captured by Tom
Burtonwood at The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Met MakerBot Hackathon, June 2012;
published by met: https://www.thingiverse.com/thing:24150
CC BY-SA 4.0: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/
Derivative geometry: coordinate normalization, simplification to 17,988 triangles,
and gilt material added by Blesscloud. This derived sculpture remains CC BY-SA 4.0.
This is a stele-form sculpture; no claim of a newly scanned freestanding statue.

maitreya.glb: "Maitreya, the Buddha of the Future", Minneapolis Institute of Art.
Source: https://www.artheonmuseum.org/artwork/maitreya-the-buddha-of-the-future-6f3413
https://images.artheonmuseum.org/ugc/sketchfab/6f341339440b4626b42e5c7148470014.glb
CC0 1.0: https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/
Derivative: transform flattening, scan simplification, normalized scale and gilt finish.

arhat.glb: "Standing Arhat, 6th C CE, China", Minneapolis Institute of Art.
Source: https://www.artheonmuseum.org/artwork/standing-arhat-6th-c-ce-china-5f209e
https://images.artheonmuseum.org/ugc/sketchfab/5f209e2a3efa4ec18fae8daea2426af9.glb
CC0 1.0: https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/
Derivative: transform flattening, scan simplification, normalized scale and gilt finish.
Two instances of this one source are used; this is not an eighteen-distinct-arhat set.
